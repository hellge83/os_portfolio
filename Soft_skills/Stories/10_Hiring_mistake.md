## Story 10. A hiring mistake

> Once, I misjudged a candidate for a Data Scientist position. He had done an excellent test assignment and performed very well in the technical interview too. Based on the interview process, he seemed like a strong mid-level specialist, and I felt that I had a good understanding of who we were hiring.
>
> But after he joined the team, the reality was very different. None of his tasks were completed properly — they were either left unfinished or done with very poor quality. It felt like we had hired a completely different person.
>
> I didn't immediately decide that it was simply a hiring mistake. We had several one-on-one meetings, discussed what exactly wasn't working, and created a plan that we hoped would help improve the situation.
>
> However, in the end, the situation didn't improve enough for me to feel that he was meeting the expectations of the role, so we decided not to pass his probation period.
>
> It was an unpleasant experience for me because the candidate had genuinely looked strong during the interview process. It taught me that even a good hiring process and a strong test assignment don't guarantee that someone will perform in the same way in a real working environment.
>
> Real work requires other skills too: independence, communication, the ability to take a task through to completion, and the ability to work with incomplete requirements.
>
> Now I try to evaluate candidates not only based on how well they solve a specific technical problem, but also on how I think they will work in a real team and handle real-world tasks.
