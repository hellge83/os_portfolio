## Story 5. Stakeholder disagreement (A/B testing)

> I don't always agree with stakeholders, and this often comes up when we are discussing A/B tests. For example, sometimes people want to measure a metric that the feature is not really expected to affect. Or they choose control metrics based on general system health rather than the specific risks of the feature. Sometimes they simply want to measure too many metrics at once, hoping that at least one of them will show a significant result.
>
> Usually, I explain why I think this approach may not be the best one and what risks or problems it could create. But I don't believe that being a Data Scientist means I automatically know the only correct answer. A stakeholder may have good reasons for wanting to do things in a particular way, so it's important for me to understand those reasons first.
>
> Then we can work together to find a solution that gives us the business result we need without creating methodological problems or making the results difficult to interpret.
>
> In general, I prefer stakeholders to tell me about the hypothesis and the goal rather than explain exactly how they think the problem should be solved. Even if someone comes to me with a ready-made technical solution, I first try to understand what problem they actually want to solve.
