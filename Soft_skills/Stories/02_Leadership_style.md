## Story 2. Leadership style

> I'm not the kind of manager who tells people what to do just because I'm their manager. I see myself more as a senior and more experienced teammate.
>
> For me, it's important that we don't do things just because I said so, or because someone higher up told me to do them. First, we need to understand why we are doing something and what result we want to achieve. Then we can discuss together how to get there in the best way. If I have more experience or have faced a similar problem before, I can suggest a direction, but it's important to me that the person understands both the problem and the goal.
>
> At the same time, I believe a good manager should stay involved in the team's work. If the team faces a problem, I should be there to help solve it. And if the team fails, I see it partly as my failure as a manager. If the team succeeds, it's our shared success.
>
> I also really like seeing people in my team become more independent over time. When they need less control and don't need to rely on their manager to solve everyday work problems, I see that as one of the signs that I'm doing my job well.
