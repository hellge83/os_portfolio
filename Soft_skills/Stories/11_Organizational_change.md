## Story 11. Organizational change — R&D team disbanded

> At some point, the company decided to disband our R&D team. For me, it was a challenging change, not so much from a technical point of view, but more as a people management challenge. I needed to explain what was happening to the team without creating unnecessary panic or uncertainty.
>
> First, I tried to understand the situation myself and see what options we had. I realized that there was a way to keep most of the team, but in a different setup — one that was more focused on solving practical product problems.
>
> I discussed this option with the LiveOps Director, and together we started putting it into practice. In the end, we managed to keep a significant part of the team. The team's responsibilities and its place in the organization changed, but we were able to keep most of the people and their expertise.
>
> For me, this was a valuable management experience because I had to deal with a change that I hadn't initiated myself, explain it to the team, and at the same time find a way not to lose the team and its expertise.
>
> It also taught me that during organizational changes, you don't always need to try to keep everything exactly as it was. Sometimes, it's more useful to understand what is really valuable about a team and find a way to preserve that value in a new structure.
