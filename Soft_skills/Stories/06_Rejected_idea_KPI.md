## Story 6. A rejected idea — KPI redesign

> Our department usually has quite broad goals for each planning period, and in most cases, these goals can be achieved through different features and experiments. Management preferred to measure our progress using general project KPIs such as ARPU, ARPDAU, conversion rate, and retention.
>
> The problem was that these metrics showed more of the overall health of the system. They were quite volatile, influenced by external factors, and didn't really show how much of the change was actually caused by our department.
>
> At some point, management faced this problem themselves. The KPIs started moving in different directions, and it became unclear why this was happening or how much different teams were actually contributing to the overall results.
>
> I suggested a different approach. For each global goal — for example, increasing ARPU or conversion rate — we could connect it to specific features that we planned to deliver during the period. Then, instead of looking only at broad KPIs, we could measure our contribution through the impact of individual initiatives.
>
> For example, if we launch a feature designed to increase ARPU, run an A/B test, and see a 5% increase in ARPU, we can directly connect that result to our contribution to the overall goal.
>
> The top managers supported the idea during a general meeting and asked us to prepare the new KPIs for the next discussion. However, at the next meeting, the group was smaller and included different people, and the decision was changed back to the old system. Even the VP of Development was not able to change it.
>
> For me, this was an interesting experience in terms of influence. In situations like this, I see my role as clearly explaining my position and showing the advantages, disadvantages, potential benefits, and risks. I also want to make sure that my point of view has actually been heard.
>
> But if management still makes a different decision, I can accept that. Leaders may have their own reasons, including reasons they are not required to share with me. At that point, my job is to accept the decision and move forward, rather than continue fighting for my idea forever.
