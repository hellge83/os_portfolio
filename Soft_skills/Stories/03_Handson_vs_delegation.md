## Story 3. Hands-on vs. delegation

> When I face a completely new problem, I usually prefer to explore it myself first. At least, I want to build an MVP and understand whether the approach actually works. It's important for me to get a deep enough understanding of a new problem instead of simply giving it to someone else and then only managing the work through status updates.
>
> But that doesn't mean I always start everything myself and then hand it over for someone else to finish. If I know that someone on the team has more experience, more time, or is simply in a better position to explore a specific part of the problem in depth, I'm happy to give them ownership.
>
> For example, I built our first churn model myself because it was our very first ML project, and I had a clear idea of how we could use it. Later, when the model needed to be improved, version 4, based on AFT, was developed by my ML Engineer.
>
> Another important point for me is that building an ML model with great Data Science metrics doesn't automatically mean that you've solved the business problem. The model also needs to be used in the right way. You need to build a product feature around its predictions — otherwise, a model is just a model. And in many cases, people with expertise outside of Data Science are better suited to this part of the work.
>
> If a task is large and can be divided into independent parts, I also prefer to distribute the work based on who is the best fit for each part.
>
> And I don't expect people to solve a problem exactly the way I would solve it. If the result is good, I accept it. Even if there are some minor weaknesses, I don't see a reason to ask someone to redo their work just because I would have approached it differently.
